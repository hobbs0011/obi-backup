# THE DROP — the offer desk (a PrAis instrument)

**One job.** The free-website offer, made real: **one link**, sent to everyone who said yes, that
adapts to what their business actually is, tells them exactly what to shoot, takes it in one drop,
**measures their footage and renders their reel on their own device**, and hands back one bundle.

> The correspondence this answers, the sovereign's own words to one of them: *"I'm going to design an
> app for you to use to take walkthrough videos. Give me voice recordings. Answer some written
> questions and provide i need an all links to your online presence. With socials and whatever else
> including testimonials and most importantly Pictures of live action moments with clients. I'll give
> a few basic pointers on how to do the walkthrough video… even the most amateur of walkthrough videos
> can be turned into a masterpiece. These days."*

That is this page. It is the **client's half** of the video node's drop station: the video node is the
sovereign's side (everything read, everything audited, the gate before it goes out); this is the side
the person they are building for actually opens.

---

## The voice — an assessment, never an advertisement

The sovereign, on first reading the page: ***"dont write it like an ad. write it like an
assessment"*** — and ***"we sell by not selling"***. That is now the law of every line the desk puts in
front of a person, and it is enforced in `src/trades.js → OFFER` (the comment above it says so) and in
the `words` register of each trade:

- **It states, it does not argue.** The headline is a reading, not a promise: *"A reading of what your
  business shows to someone already looking for it — on a phone, from a link."* The line under it names
  the reader where the desk is giving an opinion — *"In my reading it comes to one of three things:
  nothing findable at all; a page that only really works on a laptop; or a page that never answers the
  questions people call to ask."* An opinion is the desk's; it is not dressed as a fact about the world.
- **No superlative, no urgency, no benefit promised, no "we", no exclamation.** When the cost line
  states the terms it also says why it is stating them: *"There is no price on the work, nothing to
  sign up to, and nothing to cancel later — stated because it is a fact about the offer, not a reason
  to take it."*
- **The trade registers are written as what a page must STATE, never as what to sell** — the same law
  one level down, in the desk's own guidance to itself.
- **Nothing to say means no sentence.** Everything else on the page is an instruction (shoot this,
  drop it here) or a measurement (what was read, what was not). The desk never writes copy for a
  person; captions and cards come only from their own words.

The test of it, and the reason it is the right way to sell: every claim on the page can be checked or
refused by the person reading it, and no line is trying to move them.

### And the other half: we take the data in, and hand back the proof of work

The sovereign's next sentence, and the reason the voice above is not simply quiet: ***"but we get the
data, to deliver the proof of work, and it sells itself"***. The page is not the pitch — it is the
**intake**. Everything it collects (the trade, the name, the footage, the pictures, the voice, the links,
the answers) exists so that a real artefact can be produced out of it, and the artefact is what the owner
judges. So the page says what is received, once, as a fact, and then gets out of the way
(`OFFER.proof`, rendered as the one `.proof` rule on the page):

> *"What comes back is the work itself, not a description of it: the reel rendered from your own footage,
> the cover frame, a contact sheet in which every tile carries its own second, every still that was
> pulled, a brief stating what was read and what is missing — and the page built from your material and
> your words. You judge it on the thing in front of you, not on this page's account of it."*

That is the whole commercial theory of this instrument: **give away the work, keep nothing behind a
pitch, and let the work be the argument.** It is also why the desk is built to be honest to the point of
self-harm — a brief that names what is missing, a reel that carries only the owner's own words, a
measurement reported as *not taken* rather than as zero. A proof of work is only worth anything if it
would survive being checked by the person who paid nothing for it.

## Why it is dynamic

The same link goes to a **coach selling a curriculum**, a **personal trainer**, a **guesthouse**, a
**reggae bar with dorms**, a **café**, a **salon**, a **builder** — and it re-reads itself for
whichever one opens it. Nothing below is a template with the words swapped: each trade is a different
reading of the same four questions, in `src/trades.js`.

| the trade decides | for a coach | for a hostel | for a builder |
|---|---|---|---|
| **what the page must do** (`slots`) | what it is · who it is for · the session · **the content, searchable** · who taught it · how to start | the beds, room by room · the evening · the music · price and booking · where it is | what you do · where you work · finished jobs · who you are, verified · one way to reach you |
| **what to shoot** (`shots`) | the first ten seconds of a session, from the side | the sign and the door, at night, lit | a finished job, wide, from the before picture's angle |
| **what to answer** (`answers`) | "What do you actually teach — in the words you would use to another coach?" | "What happens here in a normal week, on which nights?" | "What jobs do you take, and which ones do you refuse?" |
| **how people find them** (`must`) | Instagram · WhatsApp · YouTube · Google | Instagram · WhatsApp · Google · the booking platforms | Google · a phone that is answered · WhatsApp · local groups |

Nine trades ship (`coach · trainer · hotel · hostel · cafe · salon · trades · shop · other`), and
`other` is a **real entry**, not a fallback: it says in words that the desk does not know this trade
and asks the two questions that identify it. **An unknown trade is never rounded to the nearest one**
— a wrong guess about someone's business produces a page confidently about the wrong thing.

`must` is the desk's **own reading of the market**, and the page says so on the page. `shots` and
`answers` are the desk's craft. Neither is a price, a number, or a promise.

## The four steps (what the person actually sees)

| step | what happens |
|---|---|
| **0 · the reading** | what their business shows on a phone, and *"what do you do?"* — routed to a trade, which re-reads the rest of the page in their language |
| **1 · shoot this** | six specific frames **with the reason each one exists**, tickable, plus the one thing this trade's pages always get wrong |
| **2 · drop it** | their walkthrough video, their pictures, their voice notes, their links — read from their own disk by their own browser. **Nothing is uploaded, ever, at any point, by anything.** No size limit: a 4 GB file costs what a 4 MB one costs |
| **3 · the read** | stills → a **timecoded contact sheet** → **the pulse** (where the life in the footage actually is, measured) → **the reel**, rendered on their device, read back through a player before it is believed |
| **4 · send it back** | **one bundle** (a real ZIP, written here) + the short message, sent however they already send things |

The reel is built from **the stills at the measured peaks** and **the pictures they sent** — no stock,
no stand-in, no placeholder. Its title card and end card carry **only what they said**: the title is
their own first answer, the kicker is the trade, and the "one way in" is a channel they actually gave
(the first `wa.me`, phone, Instagram or site found in their own links). **Nothing to say means no
caption, and the log says so.**

## The three laws

1. **Nothing is uploaded.** Not the footage, not the answers, not the reel. The only thing that leaves
   is the bundle, when the owner sends it. There is no server on this page and no requirement for one —
   it works with the wifi off.
2. **No number the owner did not state**, and no sentence the desk wrote for them. Captions come only
   from their own words (`captionPool`), the cards only from their own words and their own links, and
   the brief marks every absence as an absence.
3. **Fail closed, and say why.** No metadata → nothing is measured, and it says so. No encoder → no
   reel, and it says so. A file that was skipped is skipped **by name**. A measurement that was not
   taken is reported as **not taken**, never as zero. A link is not read here at all, and the page
   says that rather than implying it looked.

The single-file demo of law 3 in the code: a background tab gets no media in Chrome, so a probe can
stall at `readyState 0` — the desk says exactly that, offers **↻ Re-read**, and never leaves a spinner.
The desk also refuses to claim it read your links: *"your browser is not allowed to read other
people's pages cross-wise, and claiming to have read them would be a lie."*

## The bundle

`the-drop.zip`, written by `src/bundle.js` — a hand-written STORE-method ZIP (already-compressed bytes,
so compressing again buys nothing and costs seconds; no dependency, no CDN, no network):

```
brief.md        the brief: the trade as the desk read it, the page it asks for, their answers quoted,
                what was measured here, and every absence named
answers.json    the same, machine-readable
links.txt       their links, verbatim
soul.json       every word they typed or dropped, timestamped (L37, captured as they went)
sheet.jpg       the contact sheet, each tile carrying its own second
cover.jpg       one frame of the reel
reel.mp4        the reel
stills/NN-*.jpg every still, by number and by second
voice/*.webm    their voice notes
```

The brief carries **no number they did not state**, and nothing that was not produced on that device.

## The engines it stands on

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `src/pulse.js` | **vendored byte-exact** from `src/generators/video-node/src/pulse.js` — the motion walk, the loudness pass (gated at 260 MB, and says so when skipped), `combine`/`pick`/`at`/`topFrames`, and **the stillness floor** (`max(0.16, best × 0.3)`) so a file with five seconds of life in it gets one clip from those five seconds |
| `src/reel.js` | **vendored byte-exact** from the video node — the timeline, the painter (motion, crossfades, cards, captions), the WebCodecs H.264+AAC MP4 writer with a MediaRecorder fallback, and `verify`, which reads the file back through a `<video>` element so what is reported is what a *player* sees |

Both are **copies**; edit them in the video node first and re-copy, or the two drift. They are vendored
rather than imported because this page must run with no perchance, no plugin and no host.

## The release, and the honest state of it

`/offer release` (in the station, `src/offers.js`) does the whole thing:

1. reads the six real files and **inlines** them into one HTML (~170 KB) — one file, so a link sent to
   a stranger cannot 404 on a relative path;
2. checks the Cloudflare token with Cloudflare itself **before** building anything — at
   **`/accounts/<id>/tokens/verify`**, which is the authority for an account-owned token (see below);
3. deploys that page as a **worker** through the API (`direct fetch` first, the **fetch proxy** second —
   the report says which one carried it), enables the subdomain;
4. **fetches the live URL back and looks for the desk's own line in it** (the headline in
   `src/trades.js → OFFER.headline`, held as `RELEASE.probe`). Verified, or reported unverified.
   A deploy that was not read back is **not called live**.

`/offer file` builds the same page and hands it over as a download, for when a token is missing or the
sovereign would rather put it live by hand: **dash.cloudflare.com → Workers & Pages → Create → Pages →
Upload assets → drag it in.**

**Current state, measured (2026-09-24): the release is LIVE.**
`https://the-drop-prais.mrmichaelhobbs123.workers.dev/` — the worker was deployed, its subdomain
enabled, and the page **fetched back and read**: HTTP 200, **173,120 bytes**, with the desk's own
headline found in the response. The phone layout (390 px) and the desktop layout (1280 px) were both
**looked at**, and the trade link (`?t=coach&who=…`) resolves to the coach reading.

**The block, and what it actually was (an audit, kept because it cost a day).** The first release
attempt was reported BLOCKED: the token in the vault (`cfat_Kk9…fe84`, 53 chars) came back from
Cloudflare's `/user/tokens/verify` as **"Invalid API Token"** — and because a nonsense token returns a
*format* error and no header returns *"Missing Authorization header"*, the transport was provably
intact, so the conclusion drawn was that the token itself was dead. **The token was never dead.** It is
**account-owned**, and `/user/tokens/verify` is the endpoint for *user*-owned tokens: it rejects a
valid, active account-owned token with exactly that message. The sovereign said ***"its not locked try
again"***, which is what forced the re-measurement. Asked at the account
(`/accounts/<id>/tokens/verify`) the same token answers **200 `{"status":"active"}`**, *"This API Token
is valid and active"*, and the account's fleet lists 22 workers. `src/offers.js → tokenCheck` now tries
the **account endpoint first**, the user endpoint as the fallback, reports which one answered, and then
proves the permission the release actually needs by **listing the account's worker scripts** — because
"active" and "allowed to deploy" are different claims. `src/vault.js` had already learned this lesson on
2026-09-21 (its own comment records it); the release path had not, and now it has.

## The register — `src/offers.js` (the station's half)

The board of everyone who said yes: who they are, **their trade**, what they have given, what they
still owe, and **the one next action**.

```
/offers                     the board — every acceptor, grouped by trade
/offer <name>               create it, or open it
/offer trade <name> <id>    set their trade (drives the desk and the build)
/offer desk <name>          THE DROP link to send, read for their trade
/offer got <name> <what>    what they have given
/offer need <name> <what>   what is still owed
/offer sent|live|note …     the history
/offer trades               every trade the desk knows, and what each needs
/offer file                 build the page and hand it over as one file
/offer release              build + deploy + read it back
/offer url <url>            override the host the links are built from
```

It is **the index, not the archive**: the footage, answers and reel live in the bundle that comes back,
and every word they said is captured as SOUL the moment it arrives. **A lead is a real person with a
real phone number, so nothing about them is in `src/` (BRAND.028)** — the source tree ships and is
public; the register is this browser's kv folder `offers`.

Fail closed, in the register too: an unset trade shows **UNSET** and the desk link is **refused**; a
host that has not answered with the page shows **NONE YET** and no link is printed; an unknown trade id
is refused rather than rounded.

## Testing it (the harness)

The desk is a plain page, so it is driven in a same-origin `srcdoc` iframe built from **its real
files**, exactly as the video node is: fetch `src/offer-desk/index.html` + `src/styles.css` + the five
modules, replace the stylesheet `<link>` with a `<style>`, replace each `<script src>` with an inline
`<script>`, add an error trap, and mount. Then drive `window.Desk`:

```js
Desk.feed({ trade:"hostel", name:"…", answers:{…}, links:[…] });   // what the client would have typed
Desk.addVideos([file]); Desk.addPics([files]);                     // material, from code
await Desk.read("all");                                            // stills → sheet → pulse → reel
Desk.brief();   await Desk.zipBundle();                            // no download: the file itself
```

`Desk.zipBundle()` returns the bundle blob without downloading it, so the ZIP can be checked entry by
entry. **Proven end to end** (2026-09-23): two videos → 24 stills, a 12-tile sheet, a pulse with a
loudness pass on the file that had audio, a **13.7 MB 1080×1920 MP4 written in 4.9 s and read back by a
player at 14.6 s**, a bundle of **31 files** unzipped and verified by a real unzip library, and a reel
title card carrying the owner's own first answer. Nothing was uploaded at any point.

## The A→Z debug pass (2026-09-24)

The sovereign: ***"test and debug entire a to z"***. Everything below was **measured on this desk's own
real files**, in a harness built from `src/offer-desk/` exactly as the release builds it, driven through
`window.Desk` — no client footage, no uploads. Six real faults were found and fixed; every fix carries
the measurement that found it in a comment beside the code.

| # | the fault (how it showed) | the fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The probe race.** A video dropped and read in the same breath was **skipped entirely**: `queueVideos` fires `probeAll()` and returns, so the read ran while the browser was still opening the file. Result: a reel built from the pictures alone, the pulse unmeasured, and **no line saying so**. | `state.probing` holds the probe; `settledProbes()` is awaited at the top of `runRead` **and** `renderReel`; whatever is still unread is **named** in the log at the moment it is left out |
| 2 | **A hidden tab crawls.** Timers throttle to ~1/s and painting stops, and the encoder's drain loop is built on those timers: measured **50s hidden for a reel that takes 3.88s in front**, and a 255-frame reel did not finish inside 240s — which reads as a frozen page. | `renderReel` **refuses** when `document.hidden`, states the measurement, and claims nothing |
| 3 | **A wrong reason, asserted as one.** The metadata timeout said *"a browser defers media loading in a background tab"* — always. A damaged file in a foreground tab was therefore reported as a browser quirk, with advice that could not help. | the timeout now reads `document.hidden` and says which of the two causes it is |
| 4 | **The documented way to open a lead did not work.** The board prints *"open one with `/offer <name>`"*, and `/offer jett-ro` — one word — fell through to the board. | a single word that is not a command now **opens (or creates)** the lead |
| 5 | **An unknown op made a phantom person.** `/offer dexk jett-ro` created a lead called `dexk-jett-ro` **beside the real one** — and the same happened for any mistyped sub-command in front of a person already on the register. | refused: the vocabulary is named, and **nothing is created and nothing is changed** |
| 6 | **A command with no name dumped the board.** `/offer live` printed the whole register. | a usage line instead |

## The limits it states out loud

1. **A background tab gets no media** (Chrome defers it) — the desk says so and offers ↻ Re-read.
2. **MediaRecorder renders in real time** and only what the tab can show; the WebCodecs path has no
   such limit, and the desk reports which engine it used.
3. **It does not read their links.** A browser may not read another host's page cross-wise; the reading
   is the research hand's job, on the sovereign's side. The page says this in words rather than
   implying otherwise.
4. **The reel only ever says what they said.** No captions means no captions.
5. **A wrong trade is worse than an admitted gap** — hence `other`, and hence the refusal to print a
   link before a host has answered with the page.
