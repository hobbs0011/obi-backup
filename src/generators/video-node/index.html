◆ Drops 3 & 4 — the links, and the words
Where they already are, and what the job is. Give it every address you have for them — their site, their socials, their listing, their booking page — one per line. Each is fetched live and read in code (no model), then cross-checked, and that is where the missing links turn up: a channel their own site never links, a phone number that disagrees between their site and their listing, a channel this industry needs that was found nowhere. A page that will not load is recorded unread — never counted as absent.
✧ The conductor — research → blueprint → audit
One pass: read every link → research (what was read, and the model's knowledge of the industry, kept strictly apart and labelled) → the blueprint for the first prototype → the orders for whatever is missing, addressed to the video · image · copy hands → the audit → the gate. Perfect the content then runs those three hands for real — the image hand makes each still, the video hand cuts the footage and renders the film, the copy hand (the register) writes the words — and everything is re-audited afterwards, so an order only reads PASS when a hand actually made it. The copy audit is the register's own law checked in code: no number the offering does not state, and no register example reused — a line that breaks it is withheld and named rather than shipped. The gate is the last word: a FAIL blocks execution, and a remaining hole is named rather than waved through.
◇ The queue — many videos, one shot
Drop a whole folder's worth in. Each video is walked one at a time through the same pipeline — stills → the pulse → the sheet → the eye → the clips — and the run below does all of it in one pass. Nothing claims to know when something interesting happens until it has measured it: motion (frame-to-frame change, on a small canvas) and, when the file is small enough to read whole, loudness. The clips are cut from the measured peaks, and the eye names the moments it can actually see, in seconds, off the timecodes printed on the sheet.

🎞 Pull stills
Stills are pulled evenly across the whole file and drawn at full quality (JPEG 0.95). The sheet is the grid composite — the same thing the desktop combine_maxquality.js does, but in the browser and out of the video.
📱 Cut shorts
A short records in real time (3 × 15s ≈ 45s) because the browser can only capture what is playing — the page stays responsive, but leave the tab in front. Sound is captured from the file itself; if the browser refuses to play unmuted the clip is written silent and the log says so.
When the file has been measured (the queue run does this, or /pulse), the clips are cut from the busiest windows the numbers found — not from even spacing — and every clip prints the motion and loudness it was chosen on.
🎬 The reel — pictures into video
The other direction: a list of pictures becomes one video. Composed on this canvas, encoded by this browser (H.264 + AAC in an MP4, or VP9 + Opus in a WebM), written as a single file with motion, crossfades, a caption and a sound bed. Nothing is uploaded, and no library is fetched — only the encoder the browser already has.

🎙 The story — the marketing register, told over the pictures
No slideshow sells anything. This is the node's sales hand: it looks up what the venue actually offers (a page, read live), reads it through the house marketing register (src/brand.js → MARKET · GOSPEL 444 — the pillars, the 60% rule, the bilingual law), and writes the reel as one story: the hook, the enemy, the place, the proof, the offer, the call. Every line is burned into the picture it belongs to. Nothing is invented that the offering does not say.
