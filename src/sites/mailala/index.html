The story

From Generic to Island Escape

“Come off Bui Vien. Find the brick pillar that says keep it clear. Climb the stairs past the blue and the red — and the night starts.”

You come off Bui Vien

Lanterns strung across the street end to end, red and gold, motorbikes parked nose to tail under them, neon shopfronts on both sides. Mailala is the turn off that street.

The pillar tells you where the door is

Past a neighbouring kitchen and bar that is still serving, a brick pillar with the sign painted straight onto it — in two languages, because both are spoken here.

NO PARKING (IN FRONT OF THE BAR) — PLEASE KEEP IT CLEAR · KHÔNG ĐƯỢC ĐỂ XE TRƯỚC QUÁN BAR — GIỮ LỐI VÀO

Up the stairs

The light changes as you climb: blue on the floor, then red, then magenta smeared across the wall. A corridor, a white door, a yellow frame. Somebody lives here — tonight, somebody else is arriving with a bag.

The bar

Green and magenta neon over a wooden counter, bottles along the wall, a disco ball over the tables, tiled floor, a chair pulled out. This is the room the staircase promised.

The roof

Fairy lights over the counter, potted plants along the bar, warm bulbs in jars, the street and the rooftops below. The house's own word for it: a loud, colourful sanctuary.

The walls speak first

Mailala says what it is in paint. JAMAICAN CATERING — a green pot, a red hand, a palm tree. GOOD VIBES. BIG FLAVORS. FULL BELLY. TILL LATE. A painted band on the yellow wall. And three times, on three walls, the same rule: say no to drugs.

Your new crew

Two people at a table on the roof. Not a crowd — a pair, which is how it starts. Cheap bed, cold beer, and strangers by midnight.

The honest part

Dorms from $6.65, a private room with a balcony at $18, no curfew. And the gap, said out loud: we have no photographs of the dormitories. Ask on WhatsApp and we will send you the real ones.

Our spirit

Love Up · Bless Up

Mailala is a roof, a kitchen and a sound system before it is a bed. Come for the music on the roof, stay for the stories in the kitchen — that is the whole idea, and it has not changed.

Pham Ngu Lao, District 1 — the backpacker street, one turn off Bui Vien. Up a narrow staircase: plants, a dartboard, cold beer, and a mural of Bob Marley & The Wailers watching the room.

The kitchen runs late because the roof does. The beds are cheap because the point was never the bed. Solo travellers, crews, and couples who want a bed near the noise and a roof above it.

Questions

Before You Message Us

What time is check-in and check-out?

Check-in from 11:30 AM. Check-out by 1:00 PM. Late check-in? No problem — the rooftop bar is open late.

Do I need to book ahead?

For dorms, walk-ins are usually fine. For private rooms and weekends, book ahead on Hostelworld or on WhatsApp.

What is the cancellation policy?

Free cancellation up to 24 hours before check-in.

Is there music every night?

Every night on the roof, and live sets through the week — reggae, afrobeats, calypso and jazz. See this week's nights above.

Can I come for the food and the roof without staying?

Yes. Jerk chicken, cold beer and the rooftop are open to everyone — staying here is optional.

Is there somewhere to park?

Not in front of the bar — the house asks that the entrance is kept clear. Motorbike parking is on the street around the corner.
