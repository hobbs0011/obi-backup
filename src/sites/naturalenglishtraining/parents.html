How you will know it is working

Four things you will actually see, at home.

Week 2

They answer instead of nodding

The first change is almost never vocabulary — it is latency. The pause before they answer gets shorter. Parents notice this before the school does.

Week 3

Volume

The student starts saying English sentences at normal speaking volume instead of under their breath. Loud and imperfect beats quiet and perfect — that is the law the whole method is built on.

Day 30

The recording

The same speaking task from day one, played beside day thirty. Not a report from the coach — the student's own voice, twice, and you listen to both.

Ongoing

The 45/15 streak

A visible daily ratio. You can ask to see it at any point, and if it is not being done, the coach will tell you rather than let you keep paying for contact hours that cannot work alone.

What we will not do: promise a report card jump. School English and spoken English move on different clocks. A method that promises an exam-grade increase in four weeks is guessing about your child's teacher, their syllabus and their exam date — none of which we control.

The questions parents actually ask

Answered straight.

Will this conflict with school English?

The opposite, by design. Private sessions follow the same curriculum guides the school teaches from — Look and Global Success for English, Cambridge for Science, Close-up A1+ through B1+ for secondary. The hour reinforces the school week instead of pulling in a different direction. On the secondary guides NET does not re-teach the Vietnamese-teacher track; it adds the speaking and register the school hour has no room for.

Is there homework?

Not worksheets. The 45/15 is the “homework”: 45 minutes of input the student would have chosen anyway — a video, a podcast, a show — and 15 minutes of speaking out loud. The written exercises are done in the session, with someone who can correct the reasoning rather than just mark it wrong.

How much screen time is this?

Honest answer: the 45 minutes of input is usually on a screen or through headphones. That is a real cost and it is why the input is chosen to be something the student would genuinely enjoy rather than a language exercise to endure. The 15 minutes of output and the whole lesson are screen-free and face-to-face. There is no app to install and no extra screen-based product to buy.

Is my child safe with a male tutor in our home?

He has been teaching 1-on-1 in Vietnamese homes for years, holds a valid work permit, and schools reference him. A parent or guardian is welcome to sit in the room for as many sessions as they want — permanently, if that is what it takes. Sessions can also be held online, or with a door open and a parent in the next room. Choose whichever you prefer and it will not be treated as an insult.

My child is shy. Will this make it worse?

Shy children are the main reason NET exists. Nothing is corrected in a way that marks the child. A mistake is treated as a data discovery. The first few sessions deliberately ask for very short answers, and the tasks grow only when the student is comfortable — the mode ladder exists precisely so a Mouse is not pushed like a Cub.

What if we miss a session?

With 12 hours' notice, it moves — no charge. Shorter notice may be charged, and the coach confirms the exact rule in writing before the first paid hour so it is never a surprise. School holidays and travelling are normal and expected; tell him early and the plan bends around them.

What if it does not work?

The 100% Confidence Guarantee. A student who follows the 45/15 protocol will completely lose the fear of opening their mouth to speak English. Take the first month; if there is no massive, measurable surge in speaking velocity and confidence, the tuition is refunded in full. It is judged against the day-1 and day-30 recordings, which is the only fair way to judge it.
