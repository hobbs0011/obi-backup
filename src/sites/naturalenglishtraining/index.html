The diagnosis

The cage was built before you arrived.

Three failures, and they are one machine with three faces: the buffer, the fear that built it, and the apology that keeps it standing.

Failure 01

The Translate-in-Head Delay

Listen → translate to Vietnamese → translate back → speak. Under pressure the third step never finishes, and you freeze mid-sentence. That panic has a name in the doctrine: the Snowman Freeze.

Failure 02

Grammar First = Fear First

A red pen on every error teaches one lesson well: that speaking is dangerous. Students who learned to write first learn to be silent first.

Failure 03

The Apology Reflex

“Sorry, my English is bad.” Said before the first sentence of every meeting. It is not humility; it is a habit installed by a system that punished volume.

You are not broken. Your method was. Nothing in NET is designed to fix the student. It is designed to remove the mechanism that made them quiet.

The method — four moving parts

Not a philosophy. Arithmetic.

The 60% rule — comprehensible input

The brain acquires language fastest when it naturally understands about six words in ten. Above that ratio, the listener stops understanding and starts decoding — and learns nothing while sweating. So we pick material that is mostly understood and let the new minority arrive carried by the majority.

45/15 — the one-hour lifestyle

45 minutes of input (a podcast, a video, real listening — inside the commute or the gym) and 15 minutes of high-intensity output: shadowing, mimicry, speaking out loud under a little pressure. Homework as a chore is dead. The ratio is tracked daily.

10-5-5-3 — the anatomy of an hour

10 core words taught in real sentences, 5 idioms, 5 slang expressions that professionals actually use, and 3 games deployed at the warm-up, the middle and the finale. Phonics groundwork from the 42 core sounds of UK Jolly Phonics fixes Vietnamese phonetic interference at the root.

The AI-PET — accountability that does not forget

The brain deletes most unreinforced material within 48 hours. The AI-PET logs your exact struggle points and re-introduces them at 1 · 3 · 7 · 14 · 30 days, and it tracks the 45/15 ratio every day. Miss a day and the streak decays. Tough Love Mode is the point.

The mode ladder

We measure behaviour, not grammar.

A score on a paper tells you what a student knows. The mode tells you what they do when the room is watching. Every NET session ends with the coach naming the mode out loud.

MOUSE
0 – 40
Hesitation, whispering, apologising before speaking, the imposter voice. Most adults who say “my English is bad” live here.
CUB
41 – 79
Willing, and still waiting for permission. Gets the sentence right and says it too quietly to matter.
TIGER
80 – 100
Immediate response, real volume, ownership of the room. Wrong sometimes — and still the person others listen to.

THE IMMUTABLE LAW — LOUD & WRONG BEATS QUIET & RIGHT. EVERY SINGLE TIME.

Quiet and right is the trap: waiting for perfect grammar guarantees silence, and the real world does not carry a red pen. We train the student to weaponise the words they already own.
