Underneath every programme

The curriculum the schools actually run.

NET teaches from the same pacing guides the classrooms do, so a private hour reinforces the school week instead of competing with it. That is a design decision, and it is the reason a student's report card and their speaking both move.

School-year mapping — the books and the level each stage runs at
StageLevelBooks in playWhat the private hour adds
Grades 1–2Pre-A1 → A1Look · Global Success · Cambridge Primary ScienceEar-training, phonics (42 core sounds), naming, and the habit of answering out loud
Grades 3–5A1 → A2Look 3–5 · Global Success · Science 3–5Reading aloud with comprehension; Science vocabulary so the science lesson stops being a language problem
Grade 6Close-up A1+Close-up A1+ · Global Success · Science · CambridgeOutput on top of the syllabus: opinion, register, and the first two-minute monologue
Grade 7New Close-up A2New Close-up A2 · Global Success · ScienceSpeed and fluency drills; the postponing phrases are named and removed
Grade 8New Close Up B1New Close Up B1 · Global Success · ScienceArgument in writing and in speech; how to hold a position under interruption
Grade 9New Close Up B1+New Close Up B1+ · Global Success · ScienceExam-grade writing rebuilt from the band descriptors; presenting without a script
Grade 11–12B2 → C1IELTS materials + the student's own targetA dated band plan, timed speaking, and weekly full-speed listening
AdultA1 → C2Chosen per student — usually no textbook at allMeetings, negotiation, presenting, and the delivery work (volume, pace, eye contact)

Scope note, in the school's own terms: on the secondary guides NET teaches Close-up and Science, and Global Success is the Vietnamese-teacher track — so a private hour serves the exam rather than duplicating the classroom. Nothing here is guessed; the mapping is read off the pacing guides themselves.
