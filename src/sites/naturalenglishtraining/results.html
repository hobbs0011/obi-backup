The evidence chain

How a NET result is actually demonstrated.

Not with a five-star graphic. With two recordings, thirty days apart, of the same task — and the student's own permission to have them heard.

Day 1 — the recording

Before any teaching, the student performs a fixed speaking task and it is recorded. Unrehearsed, off the cuff, in whatever voice they currently have. It is deliberately unflattering, because a baseline that flatters is a baseline that lies.

Days 2–29 — the protocol, tracked

The 45/15 ratio, daily, on paper and in the AI-PET. The struggle points re-struck at 1 · 3 · 7 · 14 days. Every session has its 10-5-5-3 anatomy, so the student can see what was actually taught.

Day 30 — the same task again

Identical prompt, identical time limit. Both recordings played back to back in front of the student and, where the guardian paid, the guardian. Speaking velocity, volume, and hesitation are counted, not described.

The verdict, and the guarantee

If there has not been a massive, measurable surge in speaking velocity and confidence, the tuition for that first month is refunded in full. It is judged against the recording, not against the coach's opinion of himself.

What we will not donever
  • Write a testimonial and attribute it to a student who did not say it.
  • Borrow a quote from another school's page and put a Saigon name on it.
  • Use an AI-generated portrait as “one of our students”.
  • Publish a class photograph with the children's faces visible without consent.
  • Convert a real student's words into marketing copy and keep their name — the words are theirs.

This is not a moral flourish; it is a design decision. A school that will invent a review will invent a result, and a customer who cannot tell the difference is being sold, not taught.
