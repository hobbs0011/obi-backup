The story, in his terms

Sales, recruitment, a stage in his twenties — then a classroom.

The career before teaching was not a detour from it. Commercial insurance, marketing, life insurance across England, then managing recruitment in Australia for an Israeli agritech company deploying technology to farms — building and leading teams across moderation, cybersecurity, marketing and development, growing an investor community from five people to twenty thousand and raising over twenty million dollars without turning it into pump-and-dump noise.

In his early twenties he was personally selected to work alongside one of the world's best-known motivational speakers, Les Brown. That is where the delivery came from: stage presence, projection, audience psychology. The training that Vietnamese students now receive as the Tiger Voice was not invented in a classroom — it was learned in front of rooms that would let you know, immediately, if you were boring them.

Then Vietnam, and five years of it. Montessori kindergartens. Primary classrooms. Public high schools at GEMS and Galaxy, teaching IELTS to grade 10, 11 and 12. Private evening classes. Adults in their sixties who had studied English for thirty years and still whispered.

Across all of it, the same thing kept showing up: the students who had studied the longest were often the quietest. They knew the grammar. They could pass the test. And they would not open their mouths. The problem was never the student. The method had built the cage and then blamed the prisoner.

NET is the answer to that. It is built on what actually moved people — comprehensible input, daily output under mild pressure, a coach who never uses a red pen on confidence, and a schedule a working adult can actually keep.

We do not teach English. We unlock permission. That line is the doctrine, not a slogan. Everything on this site is an attempt to keep it honest.

The doctrine

Where the method actually comes from.

NET teaches by a written protocol — twelve laws, called GOSPEL 444 inside the house. Most of it is public on the method page; here is why it exists at all.

Diagnosis

Three failures, one mechanism

The translate-in-head delay, the fear that built it, and the apology reflex that keeps it standing. Fix the mechanism and the student speaks.

Method

Arithmetic, not inspiration

60% comprehension for input. 45/15 for the day. 10-5-5-3 for the hour. 1·3·7·14·30 for memory. Numbers a student can hold in their head.

Standard

Behaviour over grammar

Mouse, Cub, Tiger — a ladder read from what a student does in a room, not from what a test paper says they know.

The straight answer

Who NET is not for.

  • Students who want a lecture. The hour is mostly speaking. If you want somebody to explain grammar at you for sixty minutes, a big centre with a whiteboard will do it more cheaply.
  • Students who will not do the 45/15. The method is 45 minutes of input a day plus 15 of output. Two contact hours a week cannot replace it, and pretending otherwise would be exactly the dishonesty NET exists to avoid.
  • Anyone who needs a certificate without the work. NET does not sell certificates. It teaches English and shows you your own recordings.
  • Families who want the cheapest hour in the city. The rate is published and it is not the lowest. What you are buying is one student, one coach, and a plan built for the person.
