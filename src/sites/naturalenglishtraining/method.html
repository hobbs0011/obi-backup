Part one

The 60% rule

Quy tắc 60%: đầu vào dễ hiểu

The brain acquires language fastest when it naturally understands roughly six words in ten of what it hears. That is the Magic Zone. Below it, the listener abandons meaning for decoding: every word gets looked up, the sentence is held in working memory, and by the end nothing has been understood and less remembered.

Babies do not use dictionaries. They are not smarter than you; they simply never jammed the cognitive gears by pausing every five seconds to hunt for a word.

So the input is chosen for comprehension, not difficulty. Not the textbook chapter that defeats you — the podcast on the topic you would have read in Vietnamese anyway. The unknown minority arrives carried by the majority you already own.

Logic comes after confidence. A student who is still translating cannot also be analysing. Grammar is taught to name what the ear has already accepted, never to gate the mouth.

Part two

The 45/15 protocol — one hour, inside the life you already have

We do not add time to your week. We use the time that is already in it. That sentence is the entire product design.

Input · 45 minutespassive immersion

Absorb

A podcast, a video, a series you actually like — at the edge of your comprehension, with the subtitles on for the first pass and off for the second. On the commute, in the gym, while the rice cooks. It is input you would have consumed in Vietnamese anyway.

Chosen for you, per week, against your interests — not a generic listening exercise.

Output · 15 minuteshigh intensity

Perform

Shadowing, mimicry, and speaking out loud under a little deliberate pressure. Short, hard, daily. This is where the reflex is built and where the tiger voice is turned on — volume, pace, and no apology in it.

Recorded weekly. You hear your own before and after.

Why not two two-hour classes a weekthe honest arithmetic

Two hours twice a week is four hours of contact and 166 hours of nothing in between — and it is the 166 hours where language is actually acquired. The 45/15 turns the empty hours into the product. It is also why a student with a 50-hour working week can do this when they cannot do an evening class: the hour is already spent, it is just being spent in the wrong language.

Part three

The 10-5-5-3 anatomy of every hour

The lesson is not improvised and it is not a textbook walk. Every hour has the same four quantities in it, and the student can count them.

10

Core words

Taught strictly inside real-world context and full sentences. Never an isolated list to memorise and lose.

5

Idioms

The conversational glue textbooks refuse to teach — the phrases that make a speaker sound like a person rather than a dictionary.

5

Slang expressions

What professionals and native speakers actually use in meetings and on the street. Register matters, and it is taught, not absorbed by accident.

3

Interactive games

Deployed at the warm-up, mid-lesson and finale. Retention is driven by the game, not by the repetition of the drill.

Plus one root: Jolly Phonics. The 42 core sounds of UK Jolly Phonics are used to fix Vietnamese phonetic interference at the base — because a word pronounced from a Vietnamese mouth-shape is a word a listener has to decode, and decoding costs the speaker their authority.

Part four

The AI-PET — the accountability engine

Human memory decays. Within about 48 hours most unreinforced material is gone, and this is not a character flaw — it is the default setting. Willpower does not beat it. A schedule does.

The AI-PET is not a passive chatbot. It logs your exact Key Struggle Points — the words and structures that keep slipping — and strikes at them at mathematically optimal intervals: 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days. It also tracks the 45/15 ratio daily.

Miss a day and the streak decays. That is the design, not a bug in the design: you are either growing or dying.

Honest limit, stated: at NET's current scale the AI-PET is a tracking discipline the coach runs with the student — a schedule, a streak board, and reminders — not a finished app you install. A student app is being built. Until it ships, no one will be sold one.

    Part five

    The ladder: Mouse → Cub → Tiger

    One diagnostic, three modes. It measures behaviour, not grammar — hesitation and apology at the bottom, immediate volume and ownership of the room at the top.

    MOUSE
    0 – 40
    Whispering, apologising, the imposter voice. Reads English well and will not speak it under pressure.
    CUB
    41 – 79
    Willing and accurate, and still waiting for permission. Says the right sentence at the wrong volume.
    TIGER
    80 – 100
    Immediate response, high volume, ownership of the room. Fast, often imperfect, and always in the conversation.
    How it is read

    From what the student does in the room: how fast the answer comes, how loud it is, whether there is an apology attached, whether they interrupt or wait, and whether they hold the floor when someone else speaks over them. Not from a test paper — a paper cannot see any of it. This is why the first diagnostic is a conversation.

    How it moves

    Mode moves by reps under mild pressure, never by instruction. A student is not told to be confident; they are put in the same small uncomfortable situation often enough that confidence stops being a decision. Sessions 1–4 usually move a Cub faster than they move a Mouse — a Mouse needs the fear broken before the volume will arrive.

    Part six

    Environment beats willpower

    Willpower fails. An ecosystem sustains. You do not fight this alone.

    The safe space

    Zero judgment

    Mistakes are treated as data discoveries, not failures. Nothing said in the room is repeated outside it — not to a parent as a complaint, not to a class as an example.

    The buddy system

    Same level, weekly

    Peer practice with a partner at the same level, so the pressure is real and the stakes are small. Two learners who are both bad at English speak more than one who is good.

    The pressure test

    Real rooms

    Periodically the practice room is left behind for a real one — a market, a meeting, an event. The new tiger voice is taken out to see if it survives being heard.
