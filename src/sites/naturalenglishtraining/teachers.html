Teach the protocol, not against it.

NET is small on purpose: a roster of coaches who can hold a room, take feedback, and teach to a written standard. If you have been teaching English in Saigon and quietly disagreeing with the way the centres do it, this page is for you.

What the job actually is

The honest version, before the application form.

What you dothe work
Teach to the 10-5-5-3 anatomyEvery session has the same four quantities and the same three games. You are not asked to invent a curriculum — you are asked to teach one well.
Choose the input, per studentThe 60% rule means the listening material has to be picked for that person. That is real preparation, every week.
Run the accountabilityTrack the 45/15 ratio, keep the 1·3·7·14·30 schedule, and chase a student who is drifting before the parent notices.
Read the mode out loudMouse, Cub or Tiger, said to the student at the end of the session, with the reason. No participation trophies.
What you getthe offer
  • A written standard, so you always know what a good hour looks like
  • Students matched to your strengths rather than dropped on you by a scheduling system
  • A lead trainer who has taught for five years in Vietnamese classrooms and will watch your sessions, not just your numbers
  • Materials and lesson plans for the school-aligned tracks, so your prep goes into the student and not into recreating a syllabus
  • The option to be paid per hour with the lessons you actually want to teach — NET does not run a floor of thirty-student classes
The requirement that matters most. A coach at NET does not use a red pen on a student's confidence. If your instinct when a student makes a mistake is to stop them and correct the grammar before they finish the sentence, this is not the method and this is not the job. Everything else can be taught.

Read this first

What the reviewer is looking for.

  • Specificity about the method. “I like the 60% rule” is worth nothing. What would you change about the 45/15 for a nine-year-old who plays football every evening?
  • Evidence of being through something hard in the classroom. A class that went badly and what you changed afterwards says more than a list of schools.
  • Willingness to be observed. Sessions are watched, and feedback is given to you in writing. If that feels like surveillance rather than coaching, NET is the wrong place.
  • Care with a child's dignity. This is the one thing NET will not compromise on. A coach who humiliates a student to make the room laugh is out that day, whatever their results.
